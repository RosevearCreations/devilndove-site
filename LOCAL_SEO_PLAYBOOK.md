# Local SEO and Public Content Playbook — Build 230

This is a specialist implementation guide, not a second roadmap. Current priorities are in `PROJECT_STATUS_AND_ROADMAP.md`.

## Build 230 recurring local-search and image pass

1. Run the public HTML scan and require exactly one H1, a distinctive title, useful description, canonical and visible introductory copy on every indexable page.
2. Put words real buyers use in the title, main heading, opening copy, product facts and descriptive internal links only where they accurately describe the page.
3. Keep the main title visually unambiguous; do not style multiple headings as competing page titles.
4. Validate LocalBusiness/Organization and Product/Offer markup against visible name, address/service area, contact, price, availability, SKU, URL and approved representative images.
5. Compare the website with Google Business Profile name, categories, phone, site, hours, service/pickup area, products/services and real current photos.
6. Review Search Console queries/pages/indexing and Business Profile actions monthly. Make changes from observed gaps; do not guarantee first-page position because distance and prominence remain outside direct control.
7. Recheck phone layout, tap targets, text wrapping, image fallback and page speed after every CSS/media change.
8. Keep every missing/fallback/placeholder public asset in the D1 Visual Image Manifest and `missing_launch_images` Critical gate; only final approved representative real media may enter product structured data, real-product social previews or Business Profile workflows.
9. Keep equivalent primary content, image meaning and alternative text available on mobile and desktop. Use crawlable representative images near relevant copy and multiple accurate real-product angles where useful.
10. Treat generated editorial art as design, not local or product proof. Preserve its disclosure/provenance and never use it to imply products, premises, events, condition or customer results.

## Non-negotiable public SEO rules

- Keep **one visible H1 per public page**. H2/H3 headings must organize real content, not repeat keyword variations.
- Public title, description, visible copy, product facts, product structured data, image captions/alt text, canonical path, and social preview must agree.
- Use descriptive, content-specific alt text for real product/workshop media. Decorative placeholders use empty alt text and never count as proof imagery.
- Do not create thin pages or duplicate a product listing merely to add a keyword. A Workshop Journal story must add real material/process/care/condition context that is visible and useful.
- Public structured-data image URLs must be relevant, accessible, crawlable/indexable, and representative of the marked-up page.
- Use truthful Southern Ontario / Canadian wording only where it genuinely describes the business, pickup, workshop, or product context. Do not manufacture town/service-area claims.
- Do not use graphic placeholders, stock-like images, watermarks, misleading overlays, or invented before/after claims in Google Business Profile photo workflows.

## Build 200 publication checklist

Before releasing a Workshop Journal article or website-gallery feature, confirm:

1. The source Content Studio Blog/Gallery deliverable is explicitly Approved.
2. Every included media URL is selected and marked `public_allowed` in Content Studio.
3. The published title, summary, body, linked product path, image alt text, meta title, and meta description describe only the actual product/source media.
4. The lead image is a real, visually representative image—not a placeholder.
5. The public page has one H1; the specific article title is the visible H1 on its story view.
6. Article/ImageGallery structured data corresponds to visible content and uses the same public lead image/path.
7. The article adds real reader value: process, care, materials, condition, limits, or honest project context.
8. Review the deployed page at phone and desktop widths, then inspect the actual public API response and image URLs.
9. After sustained real publishing, use Search Console evidence before expanding publication volume or assuming indexing.

Search Console, Merchant Center, GBP photo/uploads, sitemap submission, social preview testing, indexability checks, customer privacy/consent, and any paid/automated provider integration. Record actual outcomes and failures; do not convert a local passing check into a claim that Google/platforms accepted or indexed the content.


## Build 201 CAIP content-source guardrail

CAIP recommendations, metadata scores, and future provider outputs are internal review aids only. Before any public use, Content Release Board must still verify that the visible page has useful unique copy, selected public-cleared source media, truthful title/meta/structured data, and descriptive real-image text. Do not publish a CAIP placeholder, score, inferred caption, or derived asset as a factual search claim without source and human review evidence.

## Build 221 packaging boundary
Packaging Studio is an authenticated admin tool and must remain `noindex,nofollow`. Do not create crawlable public pages for templates, label drafts or quantity tiers. Public product pages should use the final buyer-facing product identity, truthful visible price/availability, descriptive images and canonical URLs. Packaging claims must not be copied into public SEO text until they are reviewed and substantiated.


## Build 222 SEO pass

Build 222 adds admin-only Packaging and Startup routes, so they remain `noindex,nofollow` and are not added to the public sitemap. The public HTML audit continues to require one H1, a distinctive title, useful description and canonical URL on every indexable page. Packaging templates, scents and internal projects must not become thin public pages merely to create more location or product keywords. Public product structured data must use the same visible price, availability, product identity and images shown to buyers.

First-page local placement cannot be guaranteed. Continue improving actual relevance, complete and consistent business information, useful local/product content, customer reviews, links and prominence while avoiding unsupported town/service claims.
